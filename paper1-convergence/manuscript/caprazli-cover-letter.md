Dear Editor,

The information retrieval industry is currently spending $4.7 billion to rediscover a solution that UNESCO distributed for free in 1985.

I am submitting **"The Authority of Neural Scale"** for consideration as a Contributed Article. It tells the story of how the world's most advanced AI systems—from NVIDIA’s TiDAR to Microsoft’s SPANN—have hit a scaling wall, and how they are unconsciously converging on the architectural principles of **CDS/ISIS**, a forgotten system designed for developing nations.

This article is not just a critique; it is a map. I provide:
1.  **The Evidence:** A forensic analysis of eight modern systems that have independently recreated 40-year-old mechanisms.
2.  **The Theory:** A conceptual framework arguing that efficient retrieval requires separating semantic understanding (Neural) from structural organization (Symbolic).
3.  **The Solution:** A proposed "Neural-to-Symbolic Bridge" architecture that synthesizes these worlds to project 10x cost reductions.

**Note on Methodology:** This position paper presents the theoretical framework, historical convergence analysis, and economic argument. A companion technical paper containing rigorous mathematical proofs, code implementation, and specific benchmark reproduction is currently in preparation for submission to *ACM TOIS*. We believe *CACM* is the ideal venue for this high-level vision piece that challenges assumptions about innovation.

I bring a unique perspective to this synthesis, having published on unified authority files (ECDL 2003, Springer LNCS 2769). In this paper, I demonstrate how those classical principles of authority control provide the missing theoretical link to stabilize modern vector quantization, bridging the gap between historical library science and billion-scale AI.

This piece challenges the industry's obsession with "newness" and validates *CACM*'s mission to connect computing's history with its future. It is rigorous, it is timely, and it solves a billion-dollar problem with a free idea.

I look forward to your review.
