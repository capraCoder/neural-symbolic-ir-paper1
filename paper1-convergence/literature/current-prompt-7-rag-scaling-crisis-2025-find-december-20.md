### Key Findings

**Finding 1: Enterprise RAG Doesn't Scale **[^1]
The foundational paper "RAG Does Not Work for Enterprises" by Tilmann Bruckhaus (Strative, June 2024/cited throughout 2025) explicitly states RAG fails on four dimensions: scalability with massive knowledge bases, explainability for high-stakes decisions, integration with legacy systems, and control over retrieval/generation.

**Finding 2: Vector Search Cost/Maintenance Crisis **[^2]
Writer's October 2025 analysis documents that vector databases require complete recalculation and reassignment whenever new data is added—because embedding values depend on the entire dataset. Production workloads cost 20x+ more than pilots (\$100/month → \$2,000+/month for 10M vectors at cloud pricing).

**Finding 3: KNN/ANN Algorithm Limits **[^2]
Original 1951-era K-Nearest Neighbors algorithms powering vector search exhibit:

- Scalability collapse with dataset growth
- Curse of dimensionality (1536-dimensional embeddings degrade accuracy)
- Memory intensity (60GB RAM for 10M vectors, prohibitively expensive)
- Noise/outlier sensitivity causing retrieval failures

**Finding 4: Technology Plateau Acknowledged **[^3]
RAGFlow (leading RAG platform, July 2025) admits: "Although RAG-related papers continued to be published steadily in 2025, **genuine innovation in concepts and systems was notably scarce**. Has RAG technology reached a critical plateau?"

**Finding 5: Explainability/Hallucination Unsolved **[^4]
Hernández-Gutiérrez (July 2025) documents RAG remains "fundamentally constrained" for high-stakes users (judges, doctors, executives). Systems cannot measure relevance with enterprise accuracy and lack proper attribution for citations.

**Finding 6: December 2025 Conference Shift **[^5]
Agentic AI Europe 2025 (Dec 4-5, Barcelona) segments RAG as subordinate component within multi-agent architecture, not primary solution—indicating industry is moving beyond vector-based retrieval paradigm.

**Finding 7: Graph RAG Emerges as Alternative **[^6]
Instaclustr's November 2025 comparison shows graph-based RAG provides superior explainability, complex reasoning, and structured data handling—positioning it as competitive to vector approaches for enterprise use.
